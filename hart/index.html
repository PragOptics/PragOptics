HART Communication Resources

HART is the most widely used digital communication standard on the 4-20 mA loop, and it sits behind nearly every modern field instrument a technician touches. These resources explain how HART communication actually works, define the terms you run into on the job, and answer the questions that come up when you are standing at the loop. They are written by the team behind PragOptics and OmniBus, our universal HART communicator and calibration recorder.

Start here

  • What Is HART? A Field Guide to HART Communication covers the fundamentals: how digital HART rides on top of the 4-20 mA loop, HART commands, Device Descriptions and DTMs, HART-IP, and where calibration fits in.
  • HART Glossary defines 38 key terms in plain language, from HART-IP, WirelessHART, and FSK to burst mode, NE43, As-Found and As-Left, and trim.
  • HART Communication FAQ gives short, answer-first responses to common questions: how HART works, what a HART communicator is, HART versus 4-20 mA, HART-IP, WirelessHART, and As-Found / As-Left calibration.

Why these guides exist

Most HART references are written for protocol committees, not for the technician trimming a transmitter in the rain. We wrote these to be useful at the loop. The same vendor-neutral, common-practice view of HART that drives OmniBus also shapes how these pages explain the protocol, so what you read here lines up with how a real field tool behaves.

From reading about HART to working it

OmniBus is a handheld field node that speaks universal HART across manufacturers over USB HART, an on-board two-wire loop, HART-IP, and Wi-Fi. It reads 4-20 mA loop current directly on board, guides you through Configure, Calibrate, Loop Test, and Record, and turns every connection into a defensible As-Found / As-Left calibration record with finished certificates. To see how the protocol explained in these guides becomes day-to-day field work, visit the PragOptics home page.
